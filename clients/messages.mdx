Messages are text between you and one therapist. They're not a live chat and not a place to be assessed — they're for the practical things around a session, and for a question before you decide to book at all.

## Messaging a therapist before you book

You can message any therapist listed on Open Therapy without booking anything. It's free, it doesn't commit you, and you don't even need an account to send the first one.

Open their profile and use the message form there. If you're not signed in, give your name and email address with the message. Their answer comes to that address, and you can reply to it by email without ever making an account — the email also carries a link to set one up if you want the conversation in the app.

Write a line or two. One-word openers are turned away, because a therapist reading twenty of them can't do anything with "hi". Signed in, you can start up to five conversations in any 24 hours — enough to approach a shortlist, not enough to email the whole directory.

Only therapists whose listing is published and verified can be reached this way. If someone isn't taking messages, you're told so rather than sending one that goes nowhere.

<Note>
  A therapist is under no obligation to reply, and a reply isn't a booking. Some answer in an hour,
  some in a week, some not at all. If nobody answers, [browse the
  directory](/clients/browse-the-directory) rather than waiting.
</Note>

## What you can and can't send

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | Up to 4,000 characters per message |
| Attachments | PDF, JPG, PNG, WebP, Word, plain text and audio files, up to 25 MB each |
| Contact details | Not until a session has been paid for |

That last rule is worth understanding. Until you've paid for a session with that therapist, phone numbers, email addresses, links and social handles are blocked in both directions. If you include one you get the message back to edit rather than a silent failure. It exists so the first conversation stays on the platform, where the safeguards and the record are. Once you've paid for a session, the restriction lifts.

## Replying from your email inbox

Every message you get by email can be answered by replying to it. You don't have to open the app.

Each conversation has its own reply address, so your reply lands in the right thread. Write above the **Reply above this line** marker — everything below it, including the quoted email underneath, is stripped out before your message is posted.

Three things to know:

- Reply from the email address on your account. A reply sent from a different address isn't posted, because we can't tell it's you.
- Write something above the line. If everything you typed was below it, we email you back to say the reply arrived empty rather than letting it disappear.
- Email signatures usually carry a phone number. Before you've paid for a session, that trips the contact details rule — you'll get an email telling you the reply wasn't posted and why. Trim the signature and send again.

## Auto-replies

A therapist can set an automatic reply, usually to say how long they take to answer or that they're closed to new clients. It arrives a moment after your message and is labelled **Automatic reply** with the time, so it can't be mistaken for them writing back. You'll get at most one per conversation per day, however many messages you send, and a therapist can set one to switch itself off on a date.

An auto-reply is not an answer. Treat it as an acknowledgement that the message arrived.

## Read state and typing

A message shows as read once the other person opens the conversation. Opening your own thread marks their messages read for them, so it works the same way in both directions. While someone is typing a reply with the conversation open, you'll see that too.

Nobody is required to be online. There's no "last seen" and no expectation that you answer quickly.

## What notifications say

New messages reach you in two places: the bell in the app, and email.

Notification emails deliberately say that something happened, not what it said — a cancelled session, a new form, a receipt. New messages are the one exception: the email quotes the message in full, because a message you have to log in to read is a message you'll read hours late. The bell in the app shows the first 140 characters.

That means the text of a message can appear in your inbox and on your lock screen. If you share a mailbox or a device, turn **New messages** off under **Notifications** in **Settings**. The message still arrives — it just waits in the app instead of being emailed to you.

Support doesn't read your conversations. If you need us to act on something in one, quote the part that matters when you write to us.

## Reporting a message

If a message is inappropriate, or someone pushes you to move off the platform or pay outside it, write to [support@opentherapy.app](mailto:support@opentherapy.app) with the therapist's name and the date the message was sent. The conversation stays on your account, so there's nothing you need to save first.

If a message leaves you distressed, [Getting help](/getting-help) has the crisis lines. Messages aren't monitored, so nobody at Open Therapy is watching a thread for a reason to step in.
