You don't need an account to search the directory, read profiles, or shortlist therapists you like. You do need one to book, message or write anything down.

## Sign up

Sign-up proves your email address before it asks for a password: a six-digit code first, then the password.

  <img src="/images/signup.webp" width="2880" height="1800" alt="The Open Therapy signup page headed Who's this for?, offering I'm looking for therapy or I'm a therapist, with a Log in link underneath and a panel of photographs alongside" />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose who the account is for">
    On the signup page, under **Who's this for?**, pick **I'm looking for therapy**. You can add the
    therapist role later from the same login if you ever need to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your name and email">
    First name, last name and email address. If **Google** or **Apple** sign-in is available, those
    buttons sit above the form and skip the next step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your email">
    We email you a six-digit code, which **expires in 30 minutes** and works once. Enter it and select
    **Confirm email**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a password">
    At least **8 characters**, then **Create account**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  If the code hasn't arrived after a minute, select **Send a new code** and check your spam folder. The
  subject line is your code itself — for example, *"123456 is your Open Therapy code"*.
</Tip>

Once you're in, you land on the matching questionnaire. If you were part-way through a booking, you go back to that instead.

## Signing up during a booking

You can also make an account inside checkout, without leaving the page. The booking form asks for your name, your email and the six-digit code, and the card form appears underneath — see [Book a session](/clients/book-a-session).

That route skips the password so the booking isn't waiting on it. Your confirmation screen then offers to set one, and you can do it any time from **Settings**.

<Note>
  Your slot isn't held while you sign up. Nothing is reserved until you pay.
</Note>

## Sign in

Two ways, both on the login page.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Email and password">
    The email and password you set at signup. Tick **Keep me signed in** to stay signed in.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Email a code">
    Select **Use a code instead** and we'll send a six-digit code, good for 30 minutes. Useful if
    you've forgotten your password and don't want to reset it.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Frame caption="Both ways in sit on the login page, one under the other">

Forgotten your password? Select **Forgot password** and we'll email a reset link, also valid for 30 minutes.

## If you shortlisted things before signing up

Anything you did signed out is kept in your browser and moves onto your account when you sign in:

- Therapists you shortlisted
- Your answers to the matching questions
- Journal entries written in that browser

You won't lose a shortlist by signing up part-way through.

## Account emails

| | |
|---|---|
| `123456 is your Open Therapy code` | You sign up, ask for a code to sign in, or change your email |
| `Set a new password` | You request a password reset |
| `Your password was changed` | Your password changes |
| `Your email was changed` | Your email address changes |
| Security notice | A sign-in method is added or changed |

Every one of these ends with a line telling you what to do if it wasn't you.

## Changing your email

From **Settings**, under **Security**. It takes three steps, in this order: a code to your current address, then the new address, then a code to the new inbox. Nothing changes until the last code is confirmed.

## Signing out everywhere

Also under **Security** in **Settings**. It ends every session on every device, including the one you're using.

## Closing your account

**Settings** has an export of your record and, at the bottom, **Close my account**, which opens a request to our support team.

Closing removes your profile. Therapists must retain clinical notes for seven years under Australian law — see [Safety and privacy](/safety-and-privacy#your-clinical-record).
