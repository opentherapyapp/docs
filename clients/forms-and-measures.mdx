Your therapist can send you a questionnaire to fill in between sessions. They arrive in **Forms** in your workspace, and most take a couple of minutes.

## Two kinds of thing arrive here

| | |
|---|---|
| **Measures** | Standard questionnaires used across the profession — the PHQ-9, GAD-7, K10, DASS-21, WHO-5, PCL-5, EPDS, AUDIT-C, ASRS and PHQ-2. The wording and the answer options are fixed and identical for everyone |
| **Forms** | Paperwork and free-text: an intake questionnaire, a consent form, a telehealth consent, a safety plan, session feedback, or something your therapist wrote themselves |

A measure is scored. Its questions, its scoring and its severity bands come from a fixed registry in the app rather than from your therapist, so nobody can quietly change what a question asks or what a number means. Instruments that require a licence to use aren't on the platform at all.

Forms are not scored unless the therapist built scales into one.

## Filling one in

Everything due sits under **To fill in**, with a **Due now** or **Overdue** badge. Everything else is below it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open it">
    All the questions are on one page, with a count of how many you've answered. There's no wizard and no paging.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer every question">
    You can't send a partly answered measure. If you try, the questions still blank are listed and you're taken to the first one. A half-answered symptom scale has no meaningful score, which is why it isn't accepted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it">
    Click **Send it**. Nothing is saved until then — leaving the page loses what you typed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can reopen something you've already sent and change it. The button reads **Save changes**, and the edit replaces the answer for that date rather than adding a second one.

## Due dates

A form arrives with a cadence: Once, Weekly, Fortnightly, Monthly or Quarterly. Each repeat is its own occurrence with its own date, and past answers stay attached to the date they were for.

<Note>
  Due dates are not adjusted to your timezone. A form due today is due on the same calendar day for
  you and for your therapist, wherever either of you is. Session times are the opposite — those are
  always shown in your own timezone.
</Note>

A repeating form stays open until the next one comes due. A one-off stays open for a week and then reads as overdue.

Your therapist can pause an assignment or end it. Either way it stops asking, and anything you already sent stays.

## If you skip one

Nothing happens to you. There's no penalty, no chasing, and no effect on your bookings.

- The occurrence is recorded as missed, and your therapist can see that it was.
- The next one arrives on schedule.
- You can still fill in an earlier one from the menu on the card, which fills that gap rather than counting as the new one.

If a form doesn't make sense, or it's the wrong week to be answering it, tell your therapist rather than guessing. That's a legitimate answer in itself.

## Your own scores

Open the menu on a card and **What you've sent** lists the dates you answered, with the score beside each one — 12 of 27, for instance. Nothing is colour-coded, deliberately.

Open Therapy doesn't tell you what a score means. A number on a questionnaire is not a diagnosis and not a verdict on how you're doing; it's one input, and reading it is your therapist's work with you in the session. If a score worries you, ask them.

## Who sees your answers

The therapist who sent it. If you're seeing more than one therapist on Open Therapy, your measure scores are part of what any of them can read — they're your record, not one clinician's file. No other client sees anything.

You control how much of a measure a therapist sees, with the **Outcome measures** switch under **What your therapists see** in **Settings**. It's on by default.

| | |
|---|---|
| Switch on | Your treating therapists see the same scores you do, with the severity band and the trend |
| Switch off | They can still see that they sent you the form and whether you answered. The score, the severity band and any risk flag are withheld |

It's all or nothing — turning it off doesn't hide a bad week and show a good one.

Some measures include a question about self-harm. Answering above zero on one of those raises a flag with the therapist who sent it, so a person reads the response rather than plotting it on a chart. That flag also goes through the **Outcome measures** switch: if you've turned it off, they're told you answered and nothing more.

## If a question brings something up

<Warning>
  Open Therapy is a directory and booking platform. A flagged response is emailed to your therapist
  to read when they next open their work — nobody is watching answers as they arrive, and a form is
  not a way to reach anyone urgently.

  If you're in immediate danger, call **000**. To talk to someone now, call **Lifeline** on **13 11
  14** or **13YARN** on **13 92 76**, both 24 hours. More services are listed in [Getting
  help](/getting-help).
</Warning>

If something in a form is worth talking about before your next session, [message your therapist](/clients/messages) directly. That reaches a person; a form doesn't.
