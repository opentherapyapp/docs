A prompt is a question or an instruction to write against — somewhere to start when the page is blank. Prompts live in **Prompts** in your workspace and are free on every account.

## Where they come from

Three places, kept in separate sections on the page.

| | |
|---|---|
| **From Open Therapy therapists** | The catalogue. Prompts written by therapists practising on Open Therapy and published for anyone to use |
| **Suggested for you** | Prompts a therapist you're seeing has pointed you at |
| **Your prompts** | Ones you write yourself |

Every prompt is filed under one of eight categories: Starting out, Before a session, After a session, Hard days, Relationships, Gratitude, Growth, Rest. You can filter by category or use **Search prompts** to search the text.

A prompt you write is private to you, always. There's no way for a client to publish into the catalogue, so nothing you write here is ever seen by another client.

## Saving one

Use the bookmark in the corner of a prompt card, or open the prompt and click **Save**. Saved prompts collect in a **Saved** section near the top of the page so you don't have to go looking for them again.

What you've saved is yours. Your therapist can't see which prompts you saved, which you opened, or which you ignored.

## When a therapist suggests one

A therapist can suggest a prompt to you if you're their client — someone you have or have had a session with. It appears under **Suggested for you**, marked **Suggested by** their name, with a short note of up to 280 characters if they wrote one.

A suggestion is an invitation, not a task. What it looks like from their side is on [Prompts](/therapists/prompts) in the therapist pages.

- Nothing happens if you don't use it, and nothing is reported back about whether you did.
- You can clear a suggestion from the list. Clearing it doesn't notify them, and it doesn't remove the prompt itself — you can still find it in the catalogue.
- A therapist can't suggest a prompt to somebody who isn't their client.

## Turning a prompt into a journal entry

Open a prompt and click **Start an entry**. That takes you to the journal with a new entry already open and the prompt quoted at the top, so you can write underneath it.

From that point it's an ordinary journal entry. The quote is part of the text, so you can delete it, and the prompt isn't attached to the entry as a label — nothing tells anyone which prompt you started from.

The prompt and the entry are separate things. Deleting the entry doesn't touch the prompt, and clearing a suggestion doesn't touch anything you wrote.

## What your therapist can see

Nothing here, unless you choose otherwise on a journal entry.

| | |
|---|---|
| Prompts you saved | Not visible to anyone but you |
| Suggestions you cleared or ignored | Not reported back |
| Prompts you wrote | Private to you |
| Journal entries you wrote from a prompt | Only if you share that entry — see [Journal](/clients/journal) |

## If writing brings something up

Prompts in the Hard days category ask about difficult things on purpose. Nobody reads what you write, so writing is not a way to reach anyone. If you need help now, [Getting help](/getting-help) has the crisis lines, or message your therapist directly.
