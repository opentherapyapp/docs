You get two documents for a session, and they answer different questions. A **receipt** proves you paid. An **invoice** is what you claim with.

## The difference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Receipt** | Written the moment your card clears, which is at booking. References look like `OTR-2026-0093` |
| **Invoice** | Raised after the session has happened. References look like `OT-2026-0142` |

The invoice waits deliberately. Your card is charged at booking, often weeks ahead, and no fund will accept a document dated before the service it describes.

<Note>
  A late cancellation produces no invoice. The fee is a charge for a service that never happened, and the
  session ends cancelled rather than completed, so there's nothing to invoice and nothing to void later.
  The refund is recorded on the receipt instead.
</Note>

## Where they are

Open **Billing** in your workspace, at **/workspace/billing**.

**Invoices** come first — each line shows the service, the date, the therapist, the reference, the amount, and what you should get back where the therapist has recorded a rebate. **Payments** below it lists your receipts, showing what you paid net of anything refunded.

Selecting either opens the full document, with **Print or save as PDF** at the top.

## What's on them

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Reference | Quote it if you contact us |
| Issued | The document's own date, always in Sydney time so you, your therapist and your accountant read the same date |
| Status | See below |
| From | Your therapist, as the supplier |
| Billed to | You |
| Description | The service, as the therapist named it |
| Amount | What you paid. An invoice states what you actually bore, net of any refund |
| Paid with | On a receipt, the card it went through |
| GST | Nothing. A session invoice has no GST line, because health services of this kind are GST-free |

An invoice also carries the MBS item number where the service has one — that's the field Medicare reads.

<Note>
  No rebate amount is printed on the invoice itself. What Medicare gives back depends on your plan and
  your claim, neither of which we hold, and a guess on an invoice is worse than a blank.
</Note>

## Statuses

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Invoices">
    **Due**, **Processing**, **Paid**, **Void** or **Refunded**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Receipts">
    **Paid**, **Partly refunded** or **Refunded**. Money either moved or it didn't, so there's no "due"
    on a receipt — the only thing that can happen afterwards is it coming back.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

A partly refunded session — a late cancellation, for example — shows both numbers: what was paid, and what was returned.

## Using an invoice for a claim

The invoice has what Medicare, a private health fund or an NDIS plan manager needs: the practitioner, the service, the date and the amount you paid.

See [Rebates and funding](/clients/rebates-and-funding) for how to lodge each one.

<Tip>
  Save the PDF at the time rather than at tax time. It takes ten seconds and saves reconstructing a year
  of sessions later.
</Tip>

## Why the therapist's name is on it

Each therapist is the merchant of record for their own sessions, which is also why their practice name — not Open Therapy — appears on your card statement.

Open Therapy's fees are invoiced separately to the therapist and never appear on anything you're issued. See [Fees](/fees).

## Something's missing

An invoice appears once the session is marked as held. If a session you attended has none after a couple of days, write to [billing@opentherapy.app](mailto:billing@opentherapy.app) with the date and the therapist's name.
