You can review a therapist you've seen. What a review may say is constrained by Australian law, not by Open Therapy's preference.

<Info>
  Writing a review from your own account is being rolled out. Reviews already on profiles are shown as
  they are, and the screening below is what any new one goes through.
</Info>

## Why the rules exist

Section 133 of the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law* prohibits using testimonials to advertise a regulated health service. Writing whatever you like about a practitioner on your own social media is your business. A review Open Therapy chooses to publish on a therapist's profile is our advertising, and it has to comply.

The line: **you can describe the service, but not the care.**

## What you can write about

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Yes" icon="check">
    - Booking, waiting times, availability, punctuality, cancellations
    - Admin — invoices, fees, rebates, Medicare, NDIS, paperwork, reports
    - The premises, parking, accessibility, video quality
    - Their manner and how they communicated — courtesy, clarity, feeling listened to
    - Languages spoken, appointment times, location
  </Card>
  <Card title="No" icon="x">
    - Any symptom, condition, diagnosis, or your reason for attending
    - Any treatment, technique, medication, assessment or test result
    - Any statement about outcome, progress, recovery or effectiveness — including negative ones
    - Any clinical recommendation, such as suggesting them for a particular problem
    - Any claim about their clinical skill or expertise
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Warning>
  Implication counts. *"I can finally sleep through the night"* is a clinical outcome even though it names
  no diagnosis, and *"she really helped me with my anxiety"* names both a condition and an outcome.
  Neither can be published.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  A publishable review that still says something useful: *"Easy to book, ran on time every week, and the
  invoices came through the same day so my Medicare claims were straightforward. The rooms are up one
  flight of stairs with no lift."*
</Tip>

## Writing one

A rating from 1 to 5 and up to 2,000 characters of writing. Your review is published under your preferred name.

## How screening works

Every review goes through two stages, and it's only published when both agree it's clean: a blunt word list, and a model that reads it for meaning. The word list is too crude to decide anything on its own — terms like *pain* and *sleep* turn up in perfectly publishable sentences — so a hit sends the review to a person rather than rejecting it.

Nothing publishes on a failure. A missing configuration, an unreachable model, a malformed answer, an "unsure" verdict, or a word-list hit the model didn't agree with all mean the same thing: a person reads it.

## What happens next

Three outcomes.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Published" icon="check">
    *"Thanks — your review is live on the profile."* You also get an email and a notification, and so does
    the therapist — the rating, and that it's on their profile.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Not published" icon="x">
    *"We can't publish this one. Australian advertising rules stop us showing reviews that mention
    symptoms, treatment or whether therapy helped. A note about booking, admin or how you were treated is
    fine."*

    Nothing is stored, so there's nothing to appeal — rewrite it inside the boundary and submit again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="With our team" icon="clock">
    *"Thanks — your review is with our team and will appear once it's been read."*

    Anything borderline waits for a person rather than being published or thrown away. If they can't
    publish it, you get an email with the specific reason and what you can say instead.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  Screening errs firmly towards not publishing. A review waiting in a queue costs one person a day's wait.
  A wrongly published one is an offence under the National Law.
</Note>

## Ratings on profiles

A published review updates the therapist's star average and review count, which is also what the directory's **Highest rated** sort reads. Reviews show as a rating, the words, your name and the date.

## Complaints aren't reviews

A review isn't the channel for a concern about someone's conduct or clinical care — and by law it can't describe the clinical part anyway. Write to [governance@opentherapy.app](mailto:governance@opentherapy.app), or raise it with [Ahpra](https://www.ahpra.gov.au) directly.

See [Safety and privacy](/safety-and-privacy#raising-a-concern).

## Can a therapist remove a review?

No. Therapists have no moderation, reply or takedown control over reviews on their profile. Screening is Open Therapy's responsibility, applied the same way for everyone.
