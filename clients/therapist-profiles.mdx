A profile is everything a therapist wants you to know before you book, in a fixed order so you can compare two of them without hunting.

## The header

Their name, a **Shortlist** button, then a line carrying the star rating and how many reviews it rests on, their pronouns if they've given them, their discipline, and where they work — a suburb and city, or *"3 locations in Sydney"* when they work from several.

Under that comes their portrait, with any short video clips and photos of the rooms. Worth two minutes: hearing someone talk tells you more than a bio does.

## What's on the page

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="About" icon="file-text" defaultOpen>
    Their bio in their own words.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Services" icon="list">
    Each named service with its duration and price. Selecting one loads it into the booking panel, so the
    price you're reading is the price you'll pay.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How I work" icon="compass">
    The modalities they practise in — EMDR, ACT, schema therapy and the rest.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Blog" icon="newspaper">
    Posts they've written about the work. Where there are more than fit, a link takes you to the rest.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Groups they run" icon="users">
    Courses, workshops and ongoing groups they facilitate. You book a place rather than an hour — see
    [Groups and programs](/clients/groups).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Location or locations" icon="map-pin">
    Clinics with their standing days, or a service area on a map with the radius they'll travel for home
    visits.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Focus areas" icon="target">
    The specialties they list. Useful for checking your reason for coming is actually their work rather
    than something they'd accept.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Has lived" icon="heart">
    Experiences this therapist has been through themselves, not only trained in. This is the section
    [matching](/clients/find-a-therapist#how-the-ranking-works) weights hardest, and it's the therapist's
    own disclosure — nobody has to fill it in.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Also works with" icon="sparkles">
    Training and caseload beyond their headline focus.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Qualifications" icon="graduation-cap">
    Registering body, registration status, years practising, and their listed qualifications with dates.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rebates and funding" icon="badge-percent">
    Each funding type with a tick or a dash and a line of detail, plus their sliding scale where they
    publish one. See [Rebates and funding](/clients/rebates-and-funding).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Reviews" icon="star">
    The count is in the heading. Reviews cover the service — booking, admin, premises, manner — and by
    law can't discuss treatment or outcomes. See [Reviews](/clients/reviews).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  **Has lived** and **Also works with** are the load-bearing distinction on Open Therapy, and they're
  never merged. Having been through something is not the same as having treated it, and the profile keeps
  them apart so you can tell which you're reading.
</Note>

## The booking panel

On the right, and it follows you down the page as a bar once it scrolls out of view. You choose a service, then a mode, then a location, then a date and time. See [Book a session](/clients/book-a-session).

<Frame caption="Every service is named with its duration and price, so you can read the cost before you commit to anything">
  <img src="/images/profile-booking-panel.webp" width="758" height="1824" style={{ width: 379 }} alt="The booking panel listing four services, each with a duration and a price, an Online and In person toggle, a month calendar with only bookable days in black, and buttons reading Choose a service and Message Sarah" />
</Frame>

Two trust lines sit under it: **Registration checked by Open Therapy**, and for online work, **Encrypted video link for online sessions**. Under **Message Sarah** you can ask a question without booking.

The cancellation terms aren't on the profile — they're in one line at checkout, in your confirmation email, and on your session row afterwards.

On a phone there's no room for a column beside the page, so the panel becomes a bar pinned to the bottom of the screen with the therapist's name, their lowest price, and **Book**. It's the one place the usual navigation bar gets out of the way, because the bottom edge belongs to the thing you came for.

The panel changes shape depending on where the therapist is up to:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Taking clients** | The full booking panel, with a small waitlist form under it in case nothing suits |
| **Full** | A waitlist form instead of the panel |
| **Closed to new clients** | A panel saying so, with no form — a request would only be refused |
| **Care type not open yet** | A waitlist for the care type, with **Tell me when it opens** |

## Support workers are different

Disability support workers and other shift-based practitioners are engaged by the hour on a roster rather than booked by the appointment. Their panel shows a weekday hourly rate, each support type with its minimum hours, the rate bands for evenings, weekends and public holidays, their usual roster, how many days' notice they need for a new roster, and where they travel.

The button is **Request support**, not a checkout. No payment is taken, and they confirm the roster before anything is agreed.

<Note>
  These practitioners bill after each shift rather than at booking, and plan managers are billed direct.
</Note>

## Personal booking links

A therapist may hand out a link like `opentherapy.link/sarah-chen` on a card or in an email signature. It opens the same profile, minus the similar therapists at the bottom — arriving by their own link isn't the moment to be shown alternatives.

## Similar therapists

Three alternatives at the bottom of the page. Useful when someone is right in kind but wrong on price, location or availability.
