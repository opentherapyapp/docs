## Verifying therapists

Every therapist listed on Open Therapy has had their registration checked before their profile went live. Applications that don't clear are not published.

Which body holds that registration depends on the profession.

| Body | Covers |
|---|---|
| Ahpra | Psychology, psychiatry, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, osteopathy, chiropractic, podiatry |
| Speech Pathology Australia | Speech pathology |
| Dietitians Australia | Dietetics and nutrition |
| PACFA or ACA | Counselling and psychotherapy |
| ESSA | Exercise physiology |
| ANZACATA | Art therapy |
| AMTA | Music therapy |
| No board | Support work, support coordination and developmental education |

Roughly half of these professions sit outside Ahpra, so "registered" doesn't mean the same thing across the directory. Some are held by a national board with a public register; others by a professional association.

The last row is the one worth reading twice. A disability support worker, a support coordinator and a developmental educator have no board and no registration number to give. They are still verified — on a worker screening check rather than a register — and where someone is engaged as rostered hours rather than as appointments, their profile says so: it carries the line **"Worker screening checked by Open Therapy"** where a registered practitioner's says **"Registration checked by Open Therapy"**.

A profile also shows the registering body, the practitioner's registration status and their listed qualifications.

<Note>
  Verification confirms that someone is registered, or screened, to practise. It is not an endorsement
  of their clinical judgement, and it doesn't replace your own read of whether they're the right fit.
</Note>

## Your clinical record

Clinical notes, assessments and treatment records belong to your therapist and their practice, not to Open Therapy. Open Therapy stores them and does not read them, does not use them for marketing or advertising, and does not use them to train models.

- A therapist can see the records of clients they treat, and only those clients.
- Australian law requires practitioners to retain clinical notes for **seven years**. Closing your Open Therapy account removes your profile, but it does not and cannot delete notes a therapist is legally obliged to keep.
- Booking and payment records are held for seven years too, for the same reason.
- A request for a copy of your clinical notes goes to your therapist. Open Therapy can't hand over a record it doesn't own.

## A record a therapist keeps for you before you have an account

A therapist who already sees you can keep their file for you on Open Therapy — and book you in — before you have ever signed up. That file is theirs, kept here the way it would be kept in any other practice system.

What a therapist cannot do is create an account in your name. Credentials, a confirmed email address and consent are yours to give, so a record made for you has no password and no way to sign in until you make it into an account yourself and confirm the address it was filed under. That address has to match. It is the whole of what stops one person's file opening for another.

Until you do that, anything with two ends is switched off: nobody can message you, send you a form, suggest a prompt or share a safety plan. Notes, goals, documents and appointments work normally, because those are the therapist's own work.

Every appointment email sent to that address carries a link that says this isn't me, which stops Open Therapy writing to it and tells the therapist they have the wrong address.

## Payments and card data

Open Therapy never stores your card number. Payments run through Stripe, and the therapist is the merchant of record — their practice name appears on your statement, not ours.

See [Paying for sessions](/clients/paying-for-sessions) for what's charged and when.

## Messaging and video

Information on Open Therapy is encrypted in transit and at rest. Messages between a client and a therapist are stored alongside the rest of that client's file and are visible to the two of you.

Online sessions run on an encrypted video link that opens in your browser. **Sessions are never recorded** — not by you, not by your therapist, and not by Open Therapy. A recorded therapy session is a clinical record with its own consent and retention questions, and rather than answer them badly the feature doesn't exist.

One rule you'll meet in messages: phone numbers, email addresses and links can't be exchanged until a first session with that therapist has been paid for. It stops the directory being used as a lead list.

<Warning>
  Messaging is for scheduling, worksheets and short check-ins. It is **not** a crisis channel and is
  not monitored around the clock. If you're in immediate danger, call **000** or **Lifeline** on
  **13 11 14**. See [Getting help](/getting-help#crisis-support).
</Warning>

## Reviews and Australian advertising law

Section 133 of the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law* prohibits using testimonials to advertise a regulated health service. A review Open Therapy chooses to publish on a therapist's profile is our advertising, so it has to comply.

The line we apply: a review may describe the **service**, but not the **treatment**.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Allowed" icon="check">
    Booking, waiting times, availability, punctuality and cancellations. Admin — invoices, fees,
    rebates, Medicare, NDIS, paperwork. Premises, parking, accessibility and video quality. Manner and
    communication. Languages, appointment times, location.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Not allowed" icon="x">
    Any symptom, condition, diagnosis or reason for attending, named or implied. Any treatment,
    technique, medication or test result. Any statement about outcome, progress or whether it helped —
    positive or negative. Any clinical recommendation or claim about clinical skill.
  </Card>
</Columns>

Every review is screened before it appears, and implication counts: *"I can finally sleep through the night"* is a clinical outcome even though it names no diagnosis. A review is only published when the screening is clear on it. Anything clinical is refused with a reason, and anything borderline — or anything the screening couldn't decide — goes to a person to read rather than being published while nobody is looking.

See [Reviews](/clients/reviews) for what happens to a review you write, and [Reviews and advertising rules](/therapists/reviews-and-advertising-rules) for what a therapist may and may not ask for.

## Raising a concern

Every concern goes through one place: the support form at [opentherapy.app/support](https://opentherapy.app/support). Choose the topic and the form tells you what to expect before you write.

| Topic | What it's for | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical Concern | A concern about your care or your therapist | Within one business day |
| Privacy | Your data, your records or your account | Within two business days |
| Something Else | Anything that doesn't fit a topic | Within one business day |

You can also email [support@opentherapy.app](mailto:support@opentherapy.app). See [Getting help](/getting-help) for the full list of topics and the support phone line.

Two places to go that are independent of Open Therapy:

- [Ahpra](https://www.ahpra.gov.au) regulates registered health practitioners nationally, and takes complaints about conduct or clinical care directly.
- The [Office of the Australian Information Commissioner](https://www.oaic.gov.au) takes privacy complaints if you aren't satisfied with how Open Therapy has handled yours.
