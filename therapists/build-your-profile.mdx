Your profile is everything a client reads before booking. You build it in **Set up your practice**, and you edit it in the same place afterwards — the completed screen carries **Edit my answers**, which reopens the flow with your answers still in it.

## What a client sees

Your profile page is assembled from your answers, in this order.

| Section | Comes from |
|---|---|
| **About** | Your bio, your session modes and your languages |
| **Services** | Each service you've priced |
| **How I work** | Your approaches and modalities |
| **Blog** | Your published posts, if you write |
| **Groups** | Any group programs you run |
| **Location** | Each practice location you've added |
| **Focus areas** | What you work with |
| **Has lived** | Lived experiences, if you've chosen to publish them |
| **Also works with** | Experiences you work with clinically |
| **Qualifications** | Your registering body, years practising and qualifications |
| **Rebates and funding** | The funding you accept, and your sliding scale if you have one |
| **Reviews** | Reviews that have passed screening |

Beside the booking panel sit two assurances: **"Registration checked by Open Therapy"** and, for online sessions, **"Encrypted video link for online sessions"**.

## Your name and how you're addressed

Your name is prefilled from signup, and this is the one place to correct it to the form you practise under.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | Optional. Shown in the line under your name, beside your discipline |
| **Gender** | Optional, and never printed. It answers a client who is searching for one |

## Writing a bio

At least 40 characters to save, up to 900. The prompt asks for who you work with, how a session goes, and what to expect in the first few weeks.

## Lived experience and what you work with

This is the part that makes Open Therapy different from a filtered list, and the part worth spending your time on.

Eight themes cover the ground: culture, migration and belonging; family and the people who raised you; identity, gender and sexuality; neurodivergence, disability and health; faith, meaning and community; work, money and study; parenting, fertility and caring; and loss, harm and survival.

You pick the themes you hold, and each one you pick adds a short screen of specific experiences. Against each experience you can mark one of two things.

| | |
|---|---|
| **I've lived this** | You have been through it yourself |
| **I work with it** | You work with it clinically |

Leave anything that's neither blank.

The two are captured separately because clients weigh them differently, and collapsing them into one list of tags would throw away the distinction a client is actually looking for. On your profile they appear under different headings: **Has lived** — *"has been through these, not only trained in them"* — and **Also works with**.

<Warning>
  Only mark something as lived if it's true. It is the basis on which a client chooses you, and the one
  claim on your profile most likely to come up in the first session.
</Warning>

### Publishing the lived list, or keeping it back

Once you've marked at least one experience as lived, you're asked whether those should show on your public profile. Two options: **Keep to matching** or **Show on my profile**.

<Note>
  Matching works either way. Publishing shows them to every visitor; keeping them to matching shows
  them only to clients who named the same thing. Nothing about your caseload turns on the choice — an
  experience you've marked as both lived and worked with still appears under **Also works with** when
  the lived list stays private.
</Note>

### In your own words

An optional paragraph of up to 600 characters, shown to clients who named the same things. Two or three sentences is the shape of it. Something like: *"I grew up as the family's translator and know the grief of a culture you were never taught. You won't need to give me the background."*

## Focus areas and approaches

**What you work with** is what you'd take a referral for tomorrow, not everything you've ever seen. It feeds your profile's focus areas, the directory filters and matching.

**Your approaches and modalities** are a separate list, shown under **How I work**. If yours isn't there, you can write it in, and a phrase we recognise selects the existing option rather than creating a duplicate.

Be specific rather than exhaustive. A list of thirty makes it harder for the right person to recognise themselves in it, and it doesn't rank you any higher.

## Locations and how you work

Session modes are **Online**, **In person** and **Home visit**. Your profile summarises them in a line — *"Online + in person"*, or *"Online only"*.

If you see people in person, you add every place a client might turn up to. Each one takes a name, a street address, a suburb, a city, a state and a postcode, plus three things worth filling in:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Step-free access** | Level entry, an accessible bathroom and a bay to park in |
| **Getting here** | *"Five minutes from Newtown station"* |
| **Parking** | *"Metered street parking, free after 6pm"* |

There's no cap on locations, and adding your second site genuinely widens who finds you — location matching covers every place you work from, not just the first one listed.

Changing a location doesn't send your listing back for review.

You also set your timezone here. Clients see every time converted to their own. And you name the languages you can hold a session in — conversational counts if you'd be comfortable doing the work in it.
