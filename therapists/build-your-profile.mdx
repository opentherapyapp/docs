Your profile is everything a client reads before booking. Edit it under **Settings** in your workspace.

## The parts

| Section | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Photo** | A portrait. Clients scan these first. |
| **Headline** | One line under your name in the directory |
| **About** | Your bio. Shown at the top of your profile. |
| **Specialties** | Focus-area chips |
| **Qualifications** | Dated, with the awarding institution |
| **Registration** | Body, number and status. Shown as verified once checked. |
| **Locations** | Clinics, or a service area for home visits |
| **Media** | Portrait, short clips, and a photo gallery |

## Writing a bio

The bio is where a client decides whether you sound like someone they could talk to. A few things that work:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Say who you're for" icon="target">
    "I work mostly with adults in their twenties and thirties" tells a client more than a list of
    modalities.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Say what a session is like" icon="message-circle">
    Directive or exploratory, structured or not, homework or none. People are choosing an hour, not a
    technique.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Name your limits" icon="shield">
    Saying what you don't work with saves everyone a wasted first session.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Write as you speak" icon="pen-line">
    The bio and the first ten minutes of a session should sound like the same person.
  </Card>
</Columns>

A bio needs at least 40 characters to save.

## Lived experience

This is the part that makes Open Therapy different from a filtered list, and it's worth spending time on.

For each of the eight themes, you mark specific experiences as one of:

| Marking | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lived** | You have been through this yourself |
| **Worked with** | You work with it clinically |

Clients weight these differently, so they're captured separately rather than collapsed into one list of tags. A **lived** marking is the heaviest single factor in whether you appear in someone's shortlist — heavier than a clinical specialty match.

<Warning>
  Only mark something as lived if it's true. This is the basis on which a client chooses you, and it
  is the one claim on your profile that a client is likely to raise in the first session.
</Warning>

You can also write your experience in your own words. That paragraph is optional and appears on your profile.

## Media

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Short vertical clips" icon="video">
    Thirty to sixty seconds of you talking. Profiles with one get roughly twice the enquiries.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Photo gallery" icon="image">
    The rooms, the entrance, the waiting area. Useful for anyone anxious about arriving somewhere new.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  A clip doesn't need production. Say who you work with, what a first session looks like, and one
  thing you'd want a nervous person to know. Good light and clean audio matter more than the camera.
</Tip>

## Locations and how you work

| How you work | What's shown |
|---|---|
| **Clinics** | Each site with the days you're standing there |
| **Service area** | A city and the suburbs you'll travel to, drawn as a radius on the map |
| **Online only** | Your home city, so location filters still place you |

Location matching covers every clinic you work from plus everywhere you'll travel, not just your first listed address. Adding your second site genuinely widens who finds you.

Session modes are **Online**, **In person** and **Home visit**. Your profile summarises them as *"Online + in person"* or *"Online only"*.

## Focus areas and specialties

Specialties are chips, and they feed both directory filters and matching.

Be specific rather than exhaustive. A list of thirty makes it harder for the right person to recognise themselves in it, and it doesn't rank you any higher.

## Writing on Open Therapy

Under **Writing** in your workspace you can publish two kinds of post:

| Kind | For |
|---|---|
| **Post** | Writing about the work |
| **Practice update** | News — a new location, a waitlist opening, a change of hours |

Posts appear on your profile with a reading time, in Open Therapy Updates, and at their own address so you can link to them. You can save drafts and schedule.
