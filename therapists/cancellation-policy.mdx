Your cancellation policy belongs to your practice. Open Therapy enforces the one you choose rather than imposing one.

You choose it on **Availability**, and it saves the moment you pick a card.

## The four presets

| Policy | Free cancellation | Inside the window | Did not attend |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexible** | 12 hours | No fee | 50% |
| **Standard** | 24 hours | 50% | Full fee |
| **Firm** | 48 hours | 50% | Full fee |
| **Strict** | 48 hours | Full fee | Full fee |

The one-line summaries you're choosing between:

<Tip>
  The four are spread so that two differ only in the fee and two only in the window. That lets you move
  one dimension at a time — if 24 hours isn't enough notice, try Firm before reaching for Strict.
</Tip>

## Choose one before you take bookings

<Warning>
  A session booked before you've chosen a policy carries **no fee for anything** — no late fee and no
  no-show fee. A fee nobody chose is not one we'll collect on your behalf.
</Warning>

The terms are copied onto each session as it's booked. Changing your policy applies to new bookings, and a session already in the diary keeps the terms it was booked under — which is both what makes them enforceable and why you can't tighten your way into a fee on an appointment the client already agreed to.

## Three rules that aren't yours to set

  <Card title="You never charge for your own cancellation" icon="shield-check">

    not one.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cancelling after the start time is a no-show" icon="clock">
    A client who cancels once the session was due to begin pays the no-show rate, not the late rate.
    Otherwise the no-show fee is avoidable by anyone who remembers to press the button on their way past.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The boundary itself is free" icon="equal">
    Notice is measured as "at least". Exactly 24 hours before a Standard session costs nothing.
  </Card>

Only you can record a no-show, and a session can only be cancelled once — a second attempt is refused rather than re-quoted, because an hour later the same session has crossed into the no-show tier.

## Rescheduling

Moving a session is free. Always, with no cap on the number of moves.

There's nothing to charge for: a move leaves you with the hour sold and the client with the session they paid for. And there's nothing to charge it *from* — the fee is already captured against a session that's still going ahead.

What free rescheduling can't be is unlimited in *timing*, or it becomes the way to avoid a cancellation fee. Cancel an hour before a Standard session and half the fee applies; move it to next month and cancel that with a fortnight's notice and the same client pays nothing, having done the same thing in two steps.

So the notice a client needs to move a session is:

**max(your reschedule notice, the free-cancellation window of the policy the session was booked under)**

Your reschedule notice can therefore only ever ask for *more* warning than a cancellation needs, never less. Any move that's permitted leaves more notice than the cancellation policy requires, so it can't convert a chargeable cancellation into a free one.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Standard, 24-hour notice** | A client needs 24 hours to move |
| **Firm, 24-hour notice** | 48 hours, because the policy's window is longer |
| **Flexible, 24-hour notice** | 24 hours, because yours is longer |

Inside the threshold the honest answer is that the hour can no longer be moved. It can still be cancelled, on the terms the client agreed to and has already been quoted. What they're told is: *"Moving needs 24 hours notice. You can still cancel, under your therapist's policy."*

A session that was due to start can't be moved at all — *"This session was due to start, so it can't be moved."*

<Note>
  You can always move your own appointment, whatever the notice, for the same reason your own
  cancellation is never charged for.
</Note>

## What the client sees

The wording is generated from the terms actually in force, so what appears on the booking page, in the confirmation email and in the cancellation dialog is the policy being enforced rather than a description of it that was true when somebody typed it.

On **Standard**, a client reads three rows:

| | |
|---|---|
| More than 24 hours | Cancel or reschedule yourself, no fee, no conversation needed |
| Inside 24 hours | 50% of the session fee, charged to the card on file |
| Did not attend | Full fee. Rebates can't be claimed on a missed session |

On **Flexible** they read two, because a window with no fee behind it decides nothing and printing "no fee" either side of a deadline reads as a mistake rather than as generosity:

| | |
|---|---|
| Any time before it starts | Cancel or reschedule yourself, whenever you need to. No fee, nothing to explain |
| Did not attend | 50% of the fee. Rebates can't be claimed on a missed session |

In one line, Standard summarises as *"Free cancellation up to 24 hours before. Inside that, 50% of the fee."* Flexible as *"Cancel whenever you need to, no fee. A missed session is charged at 50%."*

Before confirming, a client is told the exact amount — *"\$90.00 late cancellation fee. The remaining \$90.00 goes back on your card."* — so the charge can't be dismissed by reflex. They can also give a reason, up to 500 characters, which reaches you.

## What you receive

A cancellation fee is your earnings on a session that never happened, so it settles like any other: the fee becomes the gross, and the deductions come off that rather than off the original price.

The one thing to know is that Stripe keeps its processing fee when money goes back to a client. That cost was taken against the full price, so on a smaller cancellation fee it's clamped to what the fee can carry. You net zero rather than owing us the difference.

### Worked example

A \$180.00 session on **Standard**, with a returning client, cancelled with five hours' notice.

The fee is 50% of \$180.00, so \$90.00 is retained and \$90.00 goes back on the client's card. That \$90.00 then settles as its own gross. Stripe's fee on the original charge was \$3.36 and it keeps it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Cancellation fee retained | \$90.00 |
| Card processing | −\$3.36 |
| **You receive** | **\$86.64** |

The same session with no notice, or a no-show, charges the full fee: \$180.00 settles, less \$3.36 of processing, and you receive \$176.64.

| Client cancels | You're paid | Client refunded |
|---|---|---|
| 3 days before | \$0.00 | \$180.00 |
| 5 hours before | \$86.64 | \$90.00 |
| Doesn't attend | \$176.64 | \$0.00 |
| You cancel | \$0.00 | \$180.00 |

<Note>
  Those figures assume a returning client, so no introduction fee. If it's the client's first settled
  session with you, the introduction fee comes off as well — clamped so your payout can't go below zero.
  See [Fees and earnings](/therapists/fees-and-earnings#the-two-clamps).
</Note>
