## Why reviews here look different

Section 133 of the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law* prohibits using testimonials to advertise a regulated health service. A review Open Therapy publishes on your profile is **our advertising of your service** — and yours.

The line the law draws is *clinical* content. A review may describe the **service**, but not the **treatment**.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Booking, waiting times, availability, punctuality, cancellations | Any symptom, condition, diagnosis or reason for attending |
| Admin — invoices, fees, rebates, Medicare, NDIS, paperwork, reports | Any treatment, therapeutic technique, medication, assessment or test result |
| Premises, parking, accessibility, video and telehealth quality | Any statement about outcome, progress, improvement, recovery or effectiveness |
| Manner and communication — courtesy, clarity, felt listened to | Any clinical recommendation, such as suggesting you for a particular problem |
| Practical logistics — languages spoken, appointment times, location | Any claim about your clinical skill or expertise |

<Note>
  This cuts both ways: a review can't say therapy helped, and it can't say it didn't.
</Note>

## Screening

Nothing reaches a profile until it has been screened: a word list, then a model that reads the review against the boundary above.

A review is published only when both stages agree it's clean. Every other path leaves it unpublished — a clinical verdict, an unclear verdict, a word-list hit the model didn't see, a malformed response, an unreachable model, screening not being configured at all. **There is no path that publishes on a timeout.**

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Approved** | Published on your profile. The author is told *"Thanks — your review is live on the profile."* |
| **Rejected** | Not stored at all. The author is told which kind of content the rules stop us showing, and can write a different one |
| **Escalated** | Held for a person to read. The author is told *"Thanks — your review is with our team and will appear once it's been read."* |

The reason a review is held or rejected is recorded as one or more of: mentions a symptom, condition or diagnosis; describes treatment, technique or medication; describes a clinical outcome or improvement; recommends the practitioner for a clinical need.

Implication counts. *"I can finally sleep through the night"* is an outcome even though it names no diagnosis.

The word list is blunt and is not the decider. It catches roughly one clinical mention in ten. A hit on its own never publishes and never rejects — it sends the review to a person.

## What you can't do

<Warning>
  You have **no moderation, reply or takedown control** over reviews. You can't remove one, respond
  publicly, or ask for one to be reconsidered because you disagree with it.
</Warning>

Screening is Open Therapy's responsibility and is applied identically for everyone.

Published reviews appear under **Feedback** in your workspace, next to any private session notes. When one is published you're also told — *"You've got a new review"*, with the star rating — and a link to your public profile.

## What you can do

Write to [support@opentherapy.app](mailto:support@opentherapy.app) if a published review names a diagnosis, describes treatment, or otherwise shouldn't have cleared screening. Use the same address if a review is abusive or identifies a third party.

## Don't solicit reviews

<Warning>
  Asking clients for testimonials about your clinical care can itself breach the s133 advertising
  requirements, independently of anything Open Therapy publishes. Don't ask clients to review your
  treatment, and don't offer anything in exchange for a review.
</Warning>

A written review can run to 2,000 characters, and a rating is one to five stars.

## Your rating

Your star rating and review count are recomputed from your approved reviews each time one is published. They feed **Highest rated**, one of three directory sort options alongside **Soonest available** and **Lowest price**. Two therapists with the same average are separated by the number of reviews behind it.

## Further reading

Ahpra publishes guidance on advertising a regulated health service, including the testimonial prohibition: [ahpra.gov.au](https://www.ahpra.gov.au). It applies to your own website and social media as well as your Open Therapy profile.
