## Enquiries from people who haven't booked

Anyone can write to you from your public profile. They don't need an account — a name and an email address is enough to start.

A first message from someone new creates a client record for them, with the conversation attached, and they turn up on your caseload under **No account**. They have no way to sign in until they claim that record. See [Client files](/therapists/client-files).

Limits sit on first contact so your inbox isn't a broadcast target:

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | At least 10 characters, so a knock on the door isn't a message |
| Rate, signed-in sender | Five new conversations in 24 hours |
| Rate, visitor with no account | Five enquiries in ten minutes, counted per address and per network |

You are never obliged to reply.

## Contact details

Phone numbers, email addresses and links are refused in a conversation until there's a paid session between the two of you. The message isn't sent, and the person writing it is told why, rather than having it silently disappear.

Once a session is paid for, the guard lifts and you can exchange whatever you need to.

## Writing

A message is up to **4,000 characters**. You can attach a file — PDF, PNG, JPEG, WebP, Word, plain text, MP3 or M4A.

Both sides see a typing indicator while the other is writing, and messages arrive live in an open conversation without a refresh.

## Replying from your own email

Every notification email about a conversation carries a **reply-to address unique to that thread and to you**. Reply to it from your mail client and your reply is posted into the conversation, exactly as if you'd typed it in the workspace.

The address stays the same for the life of the thread, so an old notification sitting in your inbox still works.

### The sender has to match

The token in that address is not a credential. It says which conversation and which side — nothing more. Before anything is posted, the address the reply came **from** must equal the address on file for you.

That matters because a notification email can be forwarded. A colleague who receives one and hits reply is refused, because the reply arrives from their address, not yours.

### When a reply doesn't land

| Case | What happens |
|---|---|
| The sending address doesn't match the one on file | Nothing is posted. No answer is sent — replying would confirm the address to whoever is guessing at one |
| The reply is empty after quoted text is stripped | Nothing is posted, and you get an email telling you so |
| The reply contains contact details before a paid session | Nothing is posted, and you get an email telling you so |
| The reply is an out-of-office or other automatic mail | Ignored, so two machines don't answer each other forever |
| The same delivery arrives twice | Posted once |

Quoted text below your reply is stripped, so the conversation doesn't accumulate the whole thread each time. Type above the quote.

<Note>
  The two cases that bounce back to you are the two where silence would read as "sent". Everything
  else is a machine, a stranger, or mail that can't be attributed to anyone.
</Note>

## Auto-replies

**Auto-reply** sets an automatic first answer for people who write while you're away.

| | |
|---|---|
| Message | Up to 600 characters |
| Sent | Once per conversation per 24 hours |
| Ends | On the date you set, or when you switch it off |

Use it to say when you'll answer and what someone should do if they can't wait. It's an away message, not triage.

<Warning>
  Don't put a phone number, an email address or a link in an auto-reply — the same contact-details
  rule applies, and it will be refused. If someone is in distress, an auto-reply is not the answer:
  point people to emergency services on 000 and Lifeline on 13 11 14. See
  [Getting help](/getting-help).
</Warning>

## What a notification says

The rule across Open Therapy is that a notification is a doorbell, not a delivery: it says something happened, not what it said. A review isn't quoted, a document isn't attached, a returned check-in isn't summarised.

**New messages are the deliberate exception.** The email quotes the message in full, because a notification about a message that doesn't contain the message is a worse trade than it looks — it sends someone to a browser to read two sentences, and the thing that arrives instead of a message is a message.

The in-app bell is not the exception. It shows a **140-character preview**, enough to tell one conversation from another on a screen somebody else might be able to see.

| | |
|---|---|
| Email | The full message, quoted |
| Bell | The first 140 characters |
