## Enquiries from people who haven't booked

Anyone can write to you from your public profile — a name and an email address is enough, with no account.

A first message from someone new creates a client record with the conversation attached, listed on your caseload under **No account**. They can't sign in until they claim it — see [Client files](/therapists/client-files).

Limits sit on first contact:

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | At least 10 characters |
| Rate, signed-in sender | Five new conversations in 24 hours |
| Rate, visitor with no account | Five enquiries in ten minutes, counted per address and per network |

You are never obliged to reply.

## Contact details

Phone numbers, email addresses and links are refused in a conversation until there's a paid session between the two of you. The message isn't sent, and the sender is told why. Once a session is paid for, the guard lifts.

## Writing

A message is up to **4,000 characters**. You can attach a PDF, PNG, JPEG, WebP, Word, plain text, MP3 or M4A file.

Both sides see a typing indicator, and messages arrive live in an open conversation without a refresh.

## Replying from your own email

Every notification email about a conversation carries a **reply-to address unique to that thread and to you**. Reply to it from your mail client and your reply is posted into the conversation. The address stays the same for the life of the thread, so an old notification still works.

### The sender has to match

The token in that address is not a credential; it says which conversation and which side. The address the reply comes **from** must equal the address on file for you, so a colleague replying to a forwarded notification is refused.

### When a reply doesn't land

| Case | What happens |
|---|---|
| The sending address doesn't match the one on file | Nothing is posted. No answer is sent |
| The reply is empty after quoted text is stripped | Nothing is posted, and you get an email telling you so |
| The reply contains contact details before a paid session | Nothing is posted, and you get an email telling you so |
| The reply is an out-of-office or other automatic mail | Ignored |
| The same delivery arrives twice | Posted once |

Quoted text below your reply is stripped. Type above the quote.

## Auto-replies

**Auto-reply** sets an automatic first answer for people who write while you're away.

| | |
|---|---|
| Message | Up to 600 characters |
| Sent | Once per conversation per 24 hours |
| Ends | On the date you set, or when you switch it off |

Say when you'll answer and what someone should do if they can't wait. It's an away message, not triage.

<Warning>
  Don't put a phone number, an email address or a link in an auto-reply — the contact-details rule
  applies and it will be refused. An auto-reply is not the answer for someone in distress: point
  people to emergency services on 000 and Lifeline on 13 11 14. See [Getting help](/getting-help).
</Warning>

## What a notification says

A notification says something happened, not what it said: a review isn't quoted, a document isn't attached, a returned check-in isn't summarised.

**New messages are the deliberate exception.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Email | The full message, quoted |
| Bell | The first 140 characters |
