## Two kinds of post

| | |
|---|---|
| **Post** | Writing on your subject that sits in a topic and stays useful |
| **Practice update** | Hours, rooms, groups, fees. Short, and it ages out |

The difference isn't length, it's shelf life. A post is written once and read for years. An update is true this month.

On your profile they're separated: posts appear under **Blog**, updates under **Practice updates**. Only posts carry a topic, and only posts reach the central blog.

Your writing also collects at its own address, so you can link somebody to the set rather than to one piece.

## Where a post appears

- The **Blog** section of your public profile
- The Open Therapy blog, under the topic you chose, in the feed readers can filter to therapist writing
- Search results, with your name on the byline and a link to your availability

The five topics are **Therapy 101**, **Costs & rebates**, **Anxiety & stress**, **Kids & families** and **For therapists**. A practice update can be filed under **No topic**.

## Writing one

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the type">
    Post or practice update. You can change it later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a cover photo">
    Required to publish. It leads the card and the page. Describe it in the alt field if the picture
    shows something the title doesn't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the title and standfirst">
    The standfirst is one or two sentences shown on the card and in search results. Required to
    publish, up to 400 characters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the body">
    Plain text with light markup: `##` for a heading, `-` for a bullet, `>` for a quote, and
    `[text](/link)` for a link. The word count and read time update as you type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a topic and tags">
    Three or four tags is plenty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save a draft, or publish">
    **Save draft** keeps it to yourself. **Publish** puts it live in both places at once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A draft can be anything — two paragraphs and a title will save. The bar applies to what a reader will see: a published post needs a title, a standfirst, a cover photo and at least 20 words.

**Unpublish** takes a post back to a draft. **Delete** removes it. Neither is reversible from the reader's side, so a URL that was shared stops working.

**Pin to my profile** holds one post at the top of your writing regardless of date. The **Date** field sets what the card and the byline show.

<Info>
  There's no scheduling, despite appearances. The editor offers **Save draft**, **Publish** and
  **Unpublish** and nothing else, so a future **Date** changes the date shown on the post, not when it
  goes live. Your list of posts does carry a **Scheduled** filter, and a post can sit under it reading
  *Publishes* and a date — but nothing publishes it when that date arrives. Publish it yourself on the
  day.
</Info>

## What it does for being found

A profile is a page about you. A post is a page about a question somebody is typing into a search engine, with your name on it and your availability one click away.

That's the whole mechanism, and it's why the useful post is a specific one. The question you answer three times a week in a first session. The thing clients get wrong about your discipline. What actually happens in an assessment, step by step. When someone should, and shouldn't, come to you.

Read time is calculated from the body, so a two-minute answer to a real question outperforms a thousand words of throat-clearing.

## What can't go in a post

A post you publish is Open Therapy advertising your service, and yours. Section 133 of the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law* governs it in the same way it governs [reviews](/therapists/reviews-and-advertising-rules), and the same limits apply — this time to what you write about yourself.

Don't publish:

| | |
|---|---|
| A testimonial | Not a client's words, not a paraphrase, not "one client told me" |
| A claim of superiority | "Australia's leading", "the best", "more effective than" |
| A misleading or unverifiable claim | Including a success rate, a cure rate, or a stated outcome |
| Anything creating an unreasonable expectation of benefit | A promise about how someone will feel or how long it will take |
| An inducement to use the service without a statement of terms | "Free first session" without saying what the terms are |
| Identifiable case material | A composite is still identifiable if the person would recognise themselves |
| Advice aimed at an individual | Write general information. A post cannot assess anyone |

<Warning>
  A post is public and indexed. Nothing screens it before it goes live, and there is no editorial
  review between **Publish** and the page. Keeping to the advertising rules is your professional
  obligation, and breaching them is a matter for Ahpra rather than for us.
</Warning>

Content that breaches the rules can be removed. If you're unsure whether something is a testimonial or a description of your service, treat it as a testimonial.

## Writing about distress

You may well write about suicidal thinking, self-harm, trauma or crisis — those are the questions people search for. If you do, carry the crisis lines in the post itself. Someone reading you at 2am is not going to go looking.

Emergency services on 000. Lifeline on 13 11 14. See [Getting help](/getting-help).
