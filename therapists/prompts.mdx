A prompt is a question or an instruction to write against — somewhere for a client to start when the page is blank. **Prompts** in your workspace is where you write them, and a published one carries your name, your discipline and a link to your profile into every client's prompt list.

## Writing one

Click **New prompt**. Six things make one up.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Up to 80 characters. What the card says |
| **Description** | Up to 160. One line under the title, explaining when to reach for it |
| **The prompt itself** | Up to 2,000 characters. What a client writes against |
| **Category** | One of eight, below |
| **Icon** | From a fixed set, so the catalogue stays coherent |
| **Colour** | Mint, butter, blush, sky, or plain grey |

The eight categories are **Starting out**, **Before a session**, **After a session**, **Hard days**, **Relationships**, **Gratitude**, **Growth** and **Rest**. A client can filter by any of them, so the category is how a prompt gets found rather than a label for your own filing.

A title and a body are all that's required to save. Everything else can follow.

## Private or published

Every prompt is one or the other, and you choose when you save.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Private" icon="lock">
    Yours to work on. Nobody else sees it, and it stays in **Your prompts** marked as a draft. You can
    still suggest a private prompt to your own client — useful when you've written something for one
    person and it isn't meant for the catalogue.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Published" icon="globe">
    In the catalogue every client on Open Therapy can browse, bylined with your name and discipline
    and linked to your profile. Only therapists can publish. A client can write prompts, but only
    for themselves.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Note>
  The publish check reads the role on your record rather than the one in your session, so it takes
  effect immediately rather than up to five minutes later.
</Note>

## Suggesting one to a client

Open a prompt and choose **Suggest**. It appears in that client's **Suggested for you**, marked with your name and a note of up to 280 characters if you write one.

Three rules bound it:

- **Your caseload only.** Someone you've had, or have, a session with. There's no way to suggest a prompt to a stranger.
- **They need an account.** A client you added yourself who hasn't signed up yet can't be sent one, because a prompt is something they read in their own workspace. You'll be told rather than left wondering.
- **Suggesting the same one again replaces it.** The note is overwritten and the suggestion comes back if they'd cleared it — which is what *"I meant this one, and here's why"* should do.

A suggestion is an invitation. Nothing obliges a client to use it, and nothing tells you whether they did.

## What you can't see

Deliberately, and worth knowing before you plan around it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Who saved your prompt | Not visible |
| Who wrote an entry from it | Not visible |
| Whether a client used a suggestion | Not reported |
| What anyone wrote | Never. A journal entry is private unless the client shares that entry |

Prompts carry no read counts, unlike [Writing](/therapists/writing). If you want to know whether a prompt landed, ask the client in a session.

## Withdrawing one

**Delete** behaves differently depending on whether anyone else could see it.

A **private** prompt is deleted outright. A **published** one is withdrawn from the catalogue instead of being destroyed, because an entry written against it keeps a reference to it, and the prompt is how that entry still makes sense to its author months later. Nobody new can find it; the people who already wrote against it keep something readable.

## What not to put in one

A published prompt is you, named and linked, in front of clients who haven't chosen to see you. Nothing screens it before it appears.

- **Write for anyone, not for someone.** A prompt is general writing. It can't assess a person, and it shouldn't read as though it's addressing one — that's what a message or a session is for.
- **No case material.** A composite is still identifiable if the person would recognise themselves.
- **No claims about outcomes.** The advertising limits that apply to your [posts and reviews](/therapists/reviews-and-advertising-rules) apply to a prompt with your byline on it too.

<Warning>
  Prompts in **Hard days** ask about difficult things on purpose, and nobody reads what a client
  writes — so writing is not a way for them to reach anyone. If you write one that touches suicidal
  thinking, self-harm or crisis, put the crisis lines in the prompt itself. Emergency services on
  **000**. Lifeline on **13 11 14**. See [Getting help](/getting-help).
</Warning>
