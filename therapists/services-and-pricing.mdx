You set your own prices. Open Therapy never changes them and never adds anything to what the client pays.

Your services live in **Set up your practice**, under **What do you charge?**. You reach them the same way after you're live: **Edit my answers** on your application screen reopens the flow.

## Defining a service

A practice is a short list of different appointments — an intake, an ongoing hour, the long one, the one the client doesn't attend — and each is its own length and price. You can list up to eight.

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Name** | What you call it, up to 80 characters |
| **Length** | 30, 45, 50, 60, 80, 90 or 120 minutes. An unusual length you already use is kept |
| **Fee** | What the client pays |
| **Description** | What happens in it and who it's for, up to 240 characters |

Each one becomes something a client can book.

<Note>
  The price and length are applied from what you've set when the booking is priced, so the figure at
  checkout is always the figure you configured. The browser can't supply an amount.
</Note>

### What kind of session it is

Under each service is an optional question — **What kind of session?** — with a short list of chips for your discipline. It decides which service page your appointment is listed on, alongside everyone else who offers it.

Practices name the same thing differently: *ADHD screening* here, *ADHD assessment* there. Tagging it means a client searching for one finds the other, and you keep your own wording.

Two details worth knowing:

- If you don't answer, we read a tag from the name and say so. Change it and your answer sticks.
- If nothing in the list fits, press **None of these fits** and tell us what you'd call it. It's on your profile and in search either way, and that's how the list grows.

### What clients see in search

Search results show **from \$X**, taken from your cheapest service, with its length. Clients can find you by any session on the list, not just that one.

## Directory price filters

Price is a filter, and a band only ever matches its own unit — a session fee and an hourly support rate are different products. The session bands are **under \$130**, **\$130 – \$170**, **\$170 – \$220** and **\$220+**.

Every appointment counts, not one nominated as the headline. Someone filtering under \$130 is asking who they can afford to see, so a therapist whose ongoing hour is \$120 is an answer to that even if their assessment is \$600.

## Your sliding scale

Optional, and published: a lower fee for clients on a low income, with the two numbers that make it useful stated up front — who it's for and what they pay.

You can set up to three bands, each with a household income ceiling and the fee at or under it. Household income rather than personal, because that's the figure that decides what someone can afford and the one every other means test in the country asks for.

Once you have a band, each service gets a toggle: **Offer this from \$90 on my sliding scale**. So the scale covers the appointments you choose rather than all of them.

Your profile then reads as a ladder — *"\$90 under \$40,000, \$130 under \$70,000"*.

<Note>
  Clients tell you which band applies to them when they book. Nothing is checked, and they book at that
  price without having to ask for it. Having a scale also makes you findable under the **Sliding scale**
  funding filter.
</Note>

## Initial consultations

Most practitioners set a separate initial consultation — usually longer, usually higher. It's worth doing: the first session with a new client is where the introduction fee lands, so a realistic initial fee protects your margin on it. See [Fees and earnings](/therapists/fees-and-earnings).

## Funding you accept

Four chips: **Medicare rebates**, **NDIS**, **Private health extras** and **DVA**. They appear on your profile under **Rebates and funding**, as a tick or a dash with a line of detail, and they're a directory filter.

<Warning>
  Only privately paid sessions can be booked through Open Therapy. Bulk-billed, NDIS-invoiced, DVA and
  WorkCover sessions are rejected at the database rather than booked and then mishandled. Clients pay in
  full at booking and claim their rebate themselves — your profile says so: *"Pay in full at booking. We
  email an itemised receipt to claim with."*
</Warning>

### Medicare item numbers

Say you accept Medicare rebates and each service gains an optional **Medicare item** field. Pick the item you bill rather than typing an amount: we look up the current benefit, so the figure on your profile survives the July indexation instead of going quietly wrong three months later.

The choices are filtered by your discipline and by the length above, so a 50-minute psychology appointment offers the items it could be and not the ones it couldn't. If we don't hold what you bill, choose **Another item number…** and enter the item and the rebate yourself.

<Note>
  If you keep an item that no longer suits the length, we say so rather than quietly dropping it — a
  50-minute item on a 30-minute appointment is a billing error worth seeing.
</Note>

## Shift-based support

Support work is priced by the hour across seven rate bands — weekday daytime, weekday evening, weekday night, Saturday, Sunday, public holiday and sleepover — with its own directory filters of **Under \$80 / hour** and **\$80+ / hour**, so a four-hour minimum block never gets swept into a per-session price band. Clients engage you through a request rather than a booking, and you invoice after each shift.

<Info>
  Shift pricing isn't part of the practice setup yet, so rate bands and rosters can't be entered there.
  If you work shifts, get in touch at [support@opentherapy.app](mailto:support@opentherapy.app).
</Info>

## Changing your prices

Change them whenever you like. New prices apply to new bookings. A session already booked keeps the price it was booked at, because that's the amount the client authorised on their card.
