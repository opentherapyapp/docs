**Ask a therapist** is a public question-and-answer library at `/ask`. Anyone can ask. Published therapists answer, with their name and discipline on the answer.

**Answers** in your workspace is your queue.

## How a question reaches you

<Steps>
  <Step title="Someone asks">
    They pick a topic, write a title of at least 15 characters and up to 2,000 characters of detail.
    They can ask under their first name or anonymously.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It's screened before anybody sees it">
    Two checks run before the question exists publicly. Anything describing a crisis is answered
    with crisis lines rather than filed as a question. Anything else borderline is held for a person
    to read.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Up to 25 therapists are emailed">
    Chosen by discipline, from the care category the asker picked. Only published, unbanned
    profiles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Everyone else sees it in their queue">
    Missing the email doesn't mean missing the question. Every published question in your
    disciplines sits at the top of **Answers**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Twenty-five is the ceiling on purpose. A category with two hundred therapists in it would otherwise send two hundred emails for one question, nearly all of them arriving after somebody has answered it.

## The topics are care categories

The topic picker offers **every care category, including the ones marked coming soon**. That's deliberate: a topic here has to route a question to the clinicians who can answer it, and what a therapist has is a discipline rather than an interest in a subject heading. `/ask/topic/psychology` and `/care/psychology` mean the same thing.

So you may see questions in categories that aren't open for booking yet. Answering one is still useful — the answer outlives the launch.

## Answering

Filter your queue by **Unanswered**, **Mine** or **All**. Two cards count what's waiting and what you've published.

Open a question and write. An answer is between 80 and 4,000 characters. You can see the answers already published on the question before you write yours, so you're adding to it rather than repeating it.

You can answer any published question. Nothing is assigned to you, nothing is reserved, and you can revise your own answer — saving again replaces it and it goes back through screening.

<Warning>
  Your profile has to be published before an answer can be. An unpublished profile is either
  mid-onboarding or withdrawn, and neither should put a byline on a public page.
</Warning>

## Screening before publication

Every answer is screened before it appears. Most clear immediately and are live within a minute. Anything borderline waits for a person to read it, and your queue shows it as **In review** until it does.

You'll see one of:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Published** | Live on the public page within a minute |
| **Posted, waiting on a review** | Held for a person to read |
| **Rejected** | Not published, with the reason |

There's no appeal queue and no way to push something through. Rewrite and post again.

## Answers are public, indexed and attributed

An answer carries your name, your discipline and your photo, and it links back to your profile. It's a public web page — it will be found by search, and it will be read by people who never asked anything.

That's the point of the surface. It's also the reason to write it the way you'd want a stranger to read it in two years.

## General information, not advice

You have not assessed anyone. You don't know their history, their medication, their risk or their circumstances, and one paragraph on a website is not an episode of care.

Write for the reader who finds this from a search in two years, not for the person who asked.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Explain how something generally works | Tell the asker what they have |
| Describe what an assessment usually involves | Recommend a treatment for them |
| Say what would usually prompt seeing someone | Comment on their medication |
| Say plainly what you can't answer without assessing | Imply you're now their clinician |

<Warning>
  Answering a question does not create a therapeutic relationship, and Open Therapy does not provide
  clinical care or crisis support. If a question describes someone at risk, the answer is not a
  paragraph — it's emergency services on 000 and Lifeline on 13 11 14. See
  [Getting help](/getting-help).
</Warning>

Nothing you write is a client record. Don't answer a question by describing a client, even without a name — see [Writing](/therapists/writing) for the advertising and identifiability limits, which apply here in the same way.
