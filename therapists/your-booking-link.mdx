Every therapist gets a personal address at **`opentherapy.link/your-name`**. It opens your profile, exactly as it appears in the directory.

## Why it matters commercially

**A client who arrives through your own link carries no introduction fee.** We didn't make that introduction, so we don't charge for it.

There is no platform fee and no monthly fee, so for a client of your own the only deduction on a session is card processing at Stripe's actual cost. The workspace panel says it plainly: *"Clients who book through this link carry no introduction fee. Card processing is all that comes off."*

That makes the link worth putting on your card, in your email signature, on your own website, and anywhere else you'd normally send someone.

<Note>
  Attribution is first touch. The handle is remembered for 90 days while a client is deciding, and the
  pairing is written the moment they sign in — or at the latest at checkout, because once the session row
  exists the introduction fee has been decided. Somebody who followed one therapist's link and then
  another's belongs to the first: the earlier claim is the one that actually brought them here.
</Note>

The pairing is per therapist, so it doesn't follow the client around the platform. If they later book someone else from the directory, that therapist's introduction fee applies as normal.

## Claiming your handle

Your handle is the second question in the practice setup, and it's held for you the moment you claim it. Afterwards you'll find it under **Your link** in **Settings**, where availability is checked as you type.

The rules:

- Between **3 and 30 characters**
- Lowercase letters, numbers and single hyphens
- No double hyphens, and it can't start or end with one

Anything else you type is folded into that shape rather than rejected — capitals are lowercased, spaces and punctuation become hyphens.

## If your handle isn't available

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| *"Use 3 to 30 letters, numbers and single hyphens."* | The format is wrong |
| *"That one is reserved. Try adding your discipline or suburb."* | It clashes with a page on the site — like `about`, `blog`, `browse` or `fees` |
| *"Someone already has that one."* | Another therapist holds it |
| *"That one belonged to another therapist. Pick a different name."* | It's retired. Retired names are never reissued |

Where a suffixed version would do, we offer one — **Try sarah-chen-2** — and you have to accept it rather than having it imposed.

<Tip>
  If your name is taken, add your discipline or your suburb: `sarah-chen-psychology` or
  `sarah-chen-newtown`. Both read well on a card and are easier to say over the phone than a number.
</Tip>

## Before you're verified

The address is reserved from the day you claim it, and the page behind it opens the day your registration is verified. Until then the panel reads **Your link, reserved** and the page returns nothing.

<Warning>
  Don't hand it out before you're live. Save it somewhere by all means — but the first client who taps a
  reserved link gets a dead page.
</Warning>

## Changing it later

<Warning>
  You can change your handle once every **30 days**. If you try sooner, we tell you how many days are
  left. Choose deliberately — a handle on a printed card is hard to take back.
</Warning>

Your profile moves with it, and your old link keeps working: the previous name is retired to you and redirects to the current one, so a card printed last year still gets someone to the right place. It never resolves to anyone else, and you can always take your own former name back.

## What clients see

Your profile, at your address. Nothing on it says a client came in more cheaply than another one.

## Using it well

The workspace gives you two things to copy: the link itself, and a line for your email signature — *"Book a session with me: https://opentherapy.link/your-name"*.
