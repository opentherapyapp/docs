**Clients** in your workspace is your caseload. Open anyone on it and you get their file.

## The caseload

The list shows everyone you see, with the things you'd otherwise have to open a file to find: the next session, when you last saw them, how much of a funded plan is left, and any risk flag.

Four cards sit above it — **Active clients**, **Sessions delivered**, **Average plan use** and **Risk flags**. Average plan use is worked out only across clients who have a funded total, so a private client with no allowance doesn't drag it down.

Filter with the pills. Each one carries its own count.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Currently seeing you |
| **Waitlist** | On your list, not yet booked in |
| **Discharged** | Closed |
| **Flagged** | Carrying a risk flag of monitor or elevated |
| **No account** | A record you filed, for someone who has never signed up |
| **All** | Everyone |

Search is by name.

## Adding someone

**Add a client** opens a dialog with two modes, and the difference between them matters more than the button does.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Add them">
    Someone you already see. You give a first name, optionally a last name and an email, and press
    **Add to caseload**. Nothing is sent. You get a file you can write notes in and book against
    straight away, and they get an account only if and when they make one themselves.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Invite them">
    Someone who should be using the portal from the start. You give an email, press **Send
    invitation**, and they get your booking link. They make the account. They appear on your
    caseload once they book.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

The invitation points at your own booking link, which takes no introduction fee. See [Your booking link](/therapists/your-booking-link).

## Client records for people without accounts

This is the part of the product most worth reading slowly.

You can file a record for someone you already see who has never signed up, and book them in. What you cannot do is make an *account* for them. Open Therapy refuses, deliberately: credentials, a confirmed email address and consent belong to the person they describe, and a therapist typing somebody else's address into a form is not that person agreeing to anything.

So a filed record has no password and no way to sign in. Their file is your file.

### What works, and what waits

| | |
|---|---|
| Notes, goals, documents, safety plans, appointments | Work exactly as they do for anyone else |
| Messages, forms, prompt suggestions, sharing a safety plan | Disabled until they have an account |

The banner at the top of the file says the same thing in fewer words: **Your record, not their account**. The **Message** button is there but disabled, and anything else that needs the other end refuses with the same line — *"Not until they set up their account — there's nowhere for this to arrive."* Everything held back is held back for one reason: it has two ends, and only one of them exists.

### How they take it over

<Steps>
  <Step title="You book them in">
    The next appointment you book emails them a link, provided there's an email on the record. If
    there isn't one, add it and book.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They open the link">
    It goes to `/claim/:token`. The page discloses your name and that an appointment exists. No
    dates, no client name, nothing clinical — roughly what the envelope already said.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They sign up and confirm their address">
    Claiming needs a signed-in client account whose email address is **verified and equal to the
    address on the record**. That equality is the entire safety property. A forwarded email, a
    shared inbox or a screenshot puts the link in somebody else's hands, and none of them can
    receive a code at an address that isn't theirs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The record merges into their account">
    Sessions, notes, goals, documents, invoices, receipts and the conversation all move across.
    Messaging and forms start working. Nothing you wrote is lost, and nothing you wrote becomes
    visible to them that wasn't before.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A claim link works once. A used token and an invented one get the same answer — **That link has expired.** Refusals say as little as possible on purpose.

<Note>
  A therapist account can't claim a client record, even if the address matches. Otherwise a
  practitioner who typed their own address into a record could merge that file into their own
  profile.
</Note>

<Info>
  An enquiry from someone with no account creates the same kind of record. Somebody who writes to
  you from your public profile becomes a client record with a conversation attached, and claims it
  the same way.
</Info>

## Inside a file

The header carries their name, status and any risk flag, with **Message** and **New note** beside it. Under that, three facts: **Next session**, **Last seen**, and either sessions held or plan use.

Then four tabs — **Overview**, **Notes**, **Measures** and **Documents**.

### Overview

The clinical summary, the goals, the contact panel and the latest note.

Goals are agreed with the client and tracked as a percentage. Add one with **Add a goal**, give it a label, a progress figure and an optional note. Nothing sets progress on its own — you move it.

The contact panel and the client brief show only what the client has chosen to share. Those switches are theirs, on their side, and a field they've withheld never reaches your browser rather than being hidden in it. Pronouns are always shown: they aren't a disclosure, they're how you address someone.

### Notes

A note has three sections — **Presentation**, **Intervention** and **Plan** — and belongs to one client.

<Note>
  The old standalone notes page is gone. `/workspace/notes` now redirects to your clients. A note is
  about one person, and a page listing every note in the practice had to open with a "choose a
  client" dropdown because it didn't know whose note it was. Here the client is the page.
</Note>

Six templates are available under **Templates**: CBT session, ACT session, Intake assessment, Progress review, Risk review, and Blank. Each one is prompts rather than prose — a list of questions the section wants answered, which you delete what doesn't apply from. Nothing pre-writes an observation. The framework you pick is stored on the note, so the list doesn't claim every session was CBT.

There is no signing and no draft state. A note saves itself as you type and stays editable. Changing text that was already saved files the version it replaced, and **Edit history** reads it back, newest first, alongside any addenda.

<Warning>
  Clinical notes are your record. Clients never see them. What a client sees after a session is a
  **recap** — a short summary you choose to write and share, emailed to them and shown under the
  session. It's a thing written *to* them, not a disclosure of a note written about them.
</Warning>

### Measures

Every questionnaire this client has returned, with the latest score, the change since last time, the severity band and a trend line.

Scores and bands come from the instrument, not from anything typed in. A flagged response carries a **Needs a look** badge. Select a measure to read the individual answers.

The trend line inverts where a falling score is an improvement, so a dropping PHQ-9 doesn't read as a decline.

### Documents

Everything on file for that client, and two things you can write.

| | |
|---|---|
| **GP letter** | Drafted from your notes, the scored measures and the session dates |
| **Safety plan** | Six sections, written with the client |

<Frame caption="Anything on file sits on the left, and the two things you can draft on the right">
  <img src="/images/therapist-client-documents.webp" width="2880" height="1800" alt="The Documents tab of a client file, with one document on file — a GP mental health treatment plan PDF marked as a referral — and a Write panel offering GP letter and Safety plan, noting that a letter is drafted and never sent" />
</Frame>

A GP letter is **drafted, never sent**. We hold the referring GP as a name, not an address, and a letter about someone's mental health leaves by a route you chose. The draft uses only the facts on the record — it will not invent a diagnosis, a score, a date or a medication — and where the client has withheld outcome measures, the letter is written without them. You edit it before it goes anywhere.

A safety plan has six sections, in the order they're worked through and the order they're read in a crisis:

1. Warning signs
2. What I can do on my own
3. People and places that distract me
4. People I can ask for help
5. Professionals and services
6. Making my environment safer

There is one plan per client–therapist pair, and saving replaces it. It exists on your side until you share it, and an empty plan can't be shared.

<Warning>
  Open Therapy is a directory and booking platform. It does not provide crisis support, and nothing
  in a client's file is monitored. If someone is at immediate risk, call 000. Lifeline is 13 11 14.
  See [Getting help](/getting-help).
</Warning>

## Booking a client in yourself

You can book an appointment for anyone on your caseload, including a record with no account. That skips your published availability — a 7am appointment on a day you don't open is a decision, not a mistake — but it still refuses to put you in two places at once, and it still keeps the buffer you set between sessions.

Where there's a fee, you choose whether to send a payment request with the booking. A request needs an email address to arrive at, so a record with no email is booked without one.
